The algorithms behind Science

Its world

  • Thin-film interference Light reflected from the top and bottom of a thin layer interferes, so the colour depends on its thickness and the angle you look from.
  • Fresnel reflection and Blinn–Phong highlights Surfaces reflect more at grazing angles; highlights come from the half-vector between the light and the eye.
  • Value noise and fractal Brownian motion Hashed lattice noise summed over octaves: the paper’s grain, the nebula, the ink ridges, the developer tray, the fog on history steps.
  • Domain warping Noise fed into noise, which folds the nebula into filaments.
  • Metaballs The liquid-chrome drops are one implicit surface, the sum of r² / d² from each centre; its gradient gives the normal, so drops merge as they touch.

Its live piece

  • Kepler’s third law and transit photometry TRAPPIST-1’s seven planets at their measured periods; each one crossing the star dims it by the share of its disc it covers, (Rp / R★)².

Arriving here

  • Stateless instanced particles Thousands of cubes, streamlines, sheets, film strips, sparks and ink strokes, one draw call per kind; each position is a pure function of the scroll, so scrolling back un-builds the scene exactly.
  • The Burgers vortex An exact solution of the Navier–Stokes equations: fluid drawn in toward an axis spins faster as it closes in, keeping its angular momentum, and is stretched along the axis. The cubes ride it in along an inward spiral, round the core fastest (coloured by speed, teal to orange) while it pulls them down the axis toward Science, and out again lower down, mirrored across it: a helix that tightens and stretches. The swirl starts right under the band above and travels down the page toward Science, and every cell sends one cube and receives one.
  • Perspective projection and back-face culling Cubes and sheets fly toward a camera in front of the screen; faces turned away are skipped instead of sorted.

Arriving here

The algorithms behind Photography

Its world

  • Value noise and fractal Brownian motion Hashed lattice noise summed over octaves: the paper’s grain, the nebula, the ink ridges, the developer tray, the fog on history steps.

Arriving here

  • Stateless instanced particles Thousands of cubes, streamlines, sheets, film strips, sparks and ink strokes, one draw call per kind; each position is a pure function of the scroll, so scrolling back un-builds the scene exactly.
  • Colour negative film The page is exposed onto 35 mm film: inverted, under the orange mask colour negatives carry, with sprocket holes along its edges and lines between its frames. Its frames then come up as positive prints in the developer.
  • A twisting helix (a framed curve) Each strip of film is pulled off the table along a rising helix: its frame (tangent, width, face) is carried along the curve and turned about the tangent, so the ribbon twists as it coils up the screen.
  • Damped ripples Developer ripples spread out from the line where the film lifts, dying away with distance, under the safelight.
  • Halftone screening Tone as dots on a 45° screen, each as large as the tone it stands for; the screen grows finer as the print develops.
  • Ordered dithering An 8 × 8 Bayer threshold matrix built by interleaving bits; levels rise from 2 to 32 per channel before the print reaches full tone.
  • Signed distance fields Shapes as distance functions, so their edges stay sharp at any size: the glass card, the iris blades, the maze’s corridors, the crystal on the homepage.
