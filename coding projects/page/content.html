01 Interactive research · AI

AI Power Map

A sourced, scrollable history of the models, agents, chips, companies and rules that moved the AI frontier, and redistributed strategic power.

  • 224dated moments
  • 312citations
  • Aug 21, 2026last updated
The problem

Releases, chips, company decisions and government rules each changed what AI could do. The Power Map asks which of those moments mattered, and who gained or lost leverage when they happened.

Alex’s contribution

Alex built the Power Map as a personal project: the research document and its sources, the script that turns that document into data, and the interactive explorer.

The system

A dated record of 224 moments from 2021 to August 21, 2026. Every entry answers the same six questions: what happened, what technically changed, what it meant, how people reacted at the time, who gained or lost power, and where the sources are.

The explorer adds search, year, topic and actor filters, a short “When it felt different” strip, and model-family trend lines for the MMLU and SWE-bench benchmarks.

Evidence
  • 312 citations across the 224 entries, each entry with at least one source.
  • The build script converts the Markdown record to JSON and stops unless it finds exactly 224 events, each with sources, plus 18 power-balance rows and 8 structural shifts.
  • Method notes in the research document: “open weights” means downloadable parameters, not necessarily open training data or an open-source license, and vendor benchmark and speed figures are labeled as vendor claims unless independently verified.
  • The benchmark charts carry their own caution: harnesses, prompting and evaluation methods vary, so direction matters more than small gaps.

03 Published research · Multiomics

mBONITA

Executable network models that integrate layered omics data, identify influential genes and perform topology-based pathway analysis.

  • 2023Journal of Proteome Research
  • 6other methods compared
The problem

Multiomics experiments measure one condition at several molecular layers. Pathway enrichment analysis is used to characterize signaling, but technical and biological variability between the layers limits joint analysis.

Alex’s contribution

Alex was a research assistant at the University of Rochester Medical Center from 2020 to 2021, researching biological-network analysis methods and contributing to the mBONITA package. Alex is a co-author of the 2023 paper.

The system

mBONITA, multiomics Boolean Omics Network Invariant-Time Analysis, integrates data sets that measure several molecular layers into executable Boolean network models. It runs topology-based pathway analysis on prior-knowledge networks and flags genes that are consistently modulated across the layers, weighing observed change, variance, influence over signaling and the strength of evidence for each gene.

Evidence
  • Published in the Journal of Proteome Research, 2023, volume 22, issue 5, pages 1546–1556: “Executable Network Models of Integrated Multiomics Data,” by Palshikar, Min, Crystal, Meng, Hilchey, Zand and Thakar.
  • Applied to multiomics data from RAMOS B cells treated with the immunosuppressant cyclosporine A under varying oxygen tensions, to identify pathways involved in hypoxia-mediated chemotaxis.
  • Compared by the authors with six other pathway analysis methods designed for multiomics data. According to the paper, mBONITA identifies a set of pathways with evidence of modulation across all omics layers.
