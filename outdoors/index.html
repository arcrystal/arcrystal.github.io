Earth’s Extremes

The planet at its limits

The peaks, forests, deserts, caves and deeps that set its records: three mountains of the Karakoram and the Andes, then the rest of the planet at its highest, driest, deepest and coldest.

  1. Mountain Karakoram · Pakistan–China

    K2

    8,611 m 28,251 ft

    The second-highest mountain on Earth and the hardest of the 8,000-metre peaks: first climbed in 1954, and not in winter until January 2021.

  2. Mountain Cordillera Blanca · Peru

    Alpamayo

    5,947 m 19,511 ft

    A near-perfect pyramid of fluted ice, its southwest face ribbed from summit to snowfield. A 1966 poll of climbers named it the most beautiful mountain in the world.

  3. Mountain Cordillera Blanca · Peru

    Chopicalqui

    6,354 m 20,846 ft

    A broad, heavily glaciated giant beside Huascarán, Peru’s highest peak, climbed by its southwest ridge through séracs and crevasses since 1932.

  4. Jungle Nine countries · South America

    The Amazon

    5.5 million km² of rainforest

    The largest tropical rainforest on Earth, home to about one in ten known species. Its trees breathe out so much water that they help make their own rain.

  5. Desert Chile

    Atacama

    < 1 mm of rain a year in places

    The driest non-polar desert on Earth. Some of its weather stations have never recorded rain, and its soils are close enough to Mars’s that rover teams test there.

  6. Hoodoos Utah · USA

    Bryce Canyon

    200+ freeze–thaw days a year

    The largest collection of hoodoos on Earth: limestone spires split apart by ice that freezes and thaws in their cracks on more than 200 days a year.

    My photographs of Bryce Canyon →

  7. Waterfall Canaima · Venezuela

    Angel Falls

    979 m 3,212 ft

    The tallest uninterrupted waterfall on Earth, dropping 807 m in a single fall off the edge of Auyán-tepui, a table mountain rising out of the rainforest.

  8. Volcano Hawaiʻi · USA

    Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa

    10,210 m Mauna Kea, seafloor to summit

    Measured from its base on the ocean floor, Mauna Kea is taller than Everest. Its neighbour Mauna Loa is the largest active volcano on Earth.

  9. Undersea forest Pacific Ocean

    Kelp and black coral

    60 cm of giant kelp in a day

    Giant kelp grows up to 60 cm a day into forests over 45 m tall. Far below, deep-sea black corals off Hawaiʻi have been dated to more than 4,000 years old.

  10. Cave Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng · Vietnam

    Hang Sơn Đoòng

    38.5 million m³ the largest known cave passage

    Found by a local man, Hồ Khanh, in 1990 and first explored in 2009: more than 5 km long and 200 m high, with its own jungle under collapsed skylights, and its own clouds.

  11. Ocean trench Mariana Trench · Pacific

    Challenger Deep

    10,935 m 35,876 ft below sea level

    The deepest known point in the ocean: Everest would fit inside with more than 2 km of water to spare. Far fewer people have been to the bottom than to space.

  12. Ice East Antarctica

    Vostok Station

    −89.2 °C −128.6 °F · July 21, 1983

    The coldest air ever measured at a weather station, on an ice sheet more than 3.7 km thick. Satellites have since found hollows of the plateau near −98 °C.
